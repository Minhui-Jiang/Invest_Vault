## What is counted

- **Unit:** One distinct written item on the publisher’s website. A report involving both Nvidia and Jensen Huang is counted once.
- **Subject:** Nvidia or Huang must be a substantive subject in the title, abstract, standfirst, or available text. A passing mention, stock ticker, or link alone is insufficient.
- **Sections:** No section restriction. News, investigations, market reports, profiles, written interviews, analysis, opinion, Lex/Alphaville, and substantive written newsletters are eligible. WSJ-hosted Dow Jones market reports are included.
- **Formats:** Standalone podcasts, audio, videos and their transcripts are excluded. A written article remains eligible when it also offers an optional listen button or embedded video.
- **Live coverage:** A daily written liveblog with substantive relevant reporting counts once; individual update cards are not additional reports. Twelve WSJ daily liveblog pages are included in the inventory.
- **Duplicates:** Canonical article IDs are deduplicated. Updated headlines do not create new reports. An identical opinion column republished as a newsletter is counted once. Separately edited newsletter digests count separately, even when summarizing an underlying news report.
- **Other exclusions:** Fictional satire, unrelated material and candidates without sufficient subject evidence are excluded.

## Sources and limitations

FT discovery combined its [Nvidia topic archive](https://www.ft.com/nvidia), the [Jensen Huang topic stream](https://www.ft.com/stream/5ca7302b-b782-44a0-97d7-d9d2b98262e0), their older pages, and supplemental searches. Relevant articles can be absent from topic streams, and pagination returned inconsistent cached date ranges. The list therefore cannot establish an exhaustive FT total.

WSJ discovery combined primary article/search metadata, related-story links, historical searches, and a [public headline dataset](https://github.com/twobitapps/nvidia-headlines-ethics-2026) covering part of May–November. Its [CSV](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/twobitapps/nvidia-headlines-ethics-2026/main/data/headlines.csv) was used only as a discovery list. Its audio/video entries and liveblog cards were filtered; primary WSJ metadata was checked, and additional reports were added. No sentiment labels from that dataset were used. Public archive retrieval did not support complete WSJ enumeration.

**Date qualification:** Original publication dates were used where established. Otherwise, dates come from publisher topic listings, indexed metadata, or the discovery dataset. Some reflect updates; not every original timestamp could be independently verified. The day-level dates below are audit dates for the retrieved records, not a certified publication chronology. The monthly table is consequently an incomplete retrieved-record count, not a guaranteed exact lower bound for each original-publication month.

**Interpretation:** These results answer how many qualifying written items could be identified and linked. They do not measure every mention anywhere in the publishers’ full text, and they do not establish sentiment or editorial bias. An exhaustive count would require complete publisher archive exports or a licensed full-text database covering both outlets.
