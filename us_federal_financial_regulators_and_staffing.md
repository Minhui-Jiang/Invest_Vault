# U.S. federal financial regulators: departments and staffing

Research date: **18 November 2026**.

This report covers **13 important federal bodies**: nine core financial regulators, three specialist regulatory or enforcement bodies, and the Financial Stability Oversight Council. It is a selected list, not an inventory of every federal organization with some influence over finance.

**Staffing qualification:** agencies do not consistently publish an exact count of people employed exclusively full-time. The staffing table therefore reports **actual full-time equivalents (FTEs)**, with their dates and organizational coverage, and separately provides newer employee headcounts where verified. FTEs and headcounts are different measures; neither budget authorizations nor proposed positions are presented as actual employees.

## 1. What does “independent” mean?

An **independent federal agency usually remains part of the executive branch**, even though it sits outside the Cabinet departments and has some statutory autonomy. Independence does not make it a separate branch of government or a congressional agency. The Congressional Research Service explicitly distinguishes independence from a department from independence in decision-making. [1]

The Federal Reserve needs an additional distinction: its **Board of Governors is a federal government agency**, while the **12 regional Reserve Banks have a special public–private structure**. The Board does not belong to Treasury. Congressional oversight does not make it part of the legislative branch. In a public explanation of the Fed’s status, then-Chair Jerome Powell explicitly described it as an independent agency within the executive branch. [2][3]

The classifications below describe organizational placement. They do not imply that every independent regulator has identical protections against presidential direction or removal of its leadership.

## 2. Regulators and their organizational placement

### Core financial regulators

| Federal regulator | Main financial responsibilities | Parent department or institutional home | Government status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Federal Reserve Board of Governors (Fed)** | Bank holding companies, state-member banks, certain foreign banks, and financial stability; also central-bank responsibilities. | **No Cabinet department.** Governing agency of the Federal Reserve System. | Independent federal agency; executive branch, with substantial statutory autonomy. Reserve Banks are distinct institutions within the System. |
| **Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC)** | National banks, federal savings associations, and federal branches/agencies of foreign banks. | **Department of the Treasury.** | Independent bureau **within Treasury**, hence within the executive branch. [4] |
| **Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC)** | Deposit insurance, supervision of state nonmember banks, and resolution of failed insured banks. | **No Cabinet department.** | Independent federal agency and government corporation; executive branch. [5] |
| **National Credit Union Administration (NCUA)** | Federal credit unions; share insurance and oversight of federally insured credit unions. | **No Cabinet department.** | Independent executive-branch agency, expressly established as such by statute. [6] |
| **Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)** | Securities markets, public-company disclosure, investment funds/advisers, broker-dealers, and securities-market infrastructure. | **No Cabinet department.** | Independent regulatory commission; executive branch. |
| **Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC)** | Futures, commodity options, swaps, and associated exchanges, intermediaries, and clearing organizations. | **No Cabinet department.** | Independent regulatory commission; executive branch. It is **not part of the Department of Agriculture**. |
| **Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB)** | Consumer financial products and services, including supervision of large banks and specified nonbank financial businesses. | **Independent bureau established within the Federal Reserve System**, not within Treasury. | Executive agency by statute. The Federal Reserve Board cannot generally direct its personnel, enforcement proceedings, or rules. [7][8] |
| **Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA)** | Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, and the Federal Home Loan Bank System; conservatorship responsibilities for Fannie and Freddie. | **No Cabinet department.** It is **not part of HUD**. | Independent executive-branch agency. Its 2026 report also uses the name **“U.S. Federal Housing.”** [9] |
| **Farm Credit Administration (FCA)** | Farm Credit System institutions and the Federal Agricultural Mortgage Corporation, known as Farmer Mac. | **No Cabinet department.** It is **not part of the Department of Agriculture**. | Independent executive-branch regulator. This is the U.S. FCA, not the United Kingdom’s Financial Conduct Authority. [10] |

The core-regulator selection and jurisdiction summaries follow the Congressional Research Service’s financial regulatory framework. [11] The Library of Congress also lists the principal independent regulatory agencies under its executive-branch guide. [12]

## 3. Staffing: actual FTEs and separately reported headcounts

**How to read the table:** an annual FTE measures employment over a reporting year, not the number of people on the payroll on the last day. Two half-time employees can represent one FTE; a full-time employee employed for only half the year can also contribute roughly half an annual FTE. Some agencies instead report a point-in-time FTE based on scheduled hours. The table identifies these differences.

**FY 2025** generally means **1 October 2024–30 September 2025**; **CY 2025** means **1 January–31 December 2025**. These are the actual figures verified in the cited publications, **not a simultaneous employee census on 18 November 2026**. Blank headcount entries mean no newer count is supplied here, not zero employees.

### Important coverage notes

- **Federal Reserve:** the same CY 2024 source reports **24,179 FTEs for the entire System**, comprising the Board, OIG, Reserve Banks, and currency-related staffing. The **3,316** above is included in that System total. Reserve Bank employment should not be described as ordinary federal-agency employment, and the System total covers monetary policy, payments, and other functions in addition to regulation. [18]
- **OIG** means **Office of Inspector General**. Where a separate OIG staffing line is available, the table makes its treatment explicit. An OIG audits and investigates its agency; it is not itself the principal regulator of financial firms.
- **OFAC:** Treasury’s recent budget reports staffing for the broader **TFI organization**, which includes several offices. Assigning TFI’s entire workforce to OFAC would overstate OFAC staffing. The 204 figure is retained only as a clearly dated historical reference, not as an estimate of today’s workforce. [14][15]
- **Annual FTEs can exceed a later headcount** when staff leave during the year. This helps explain why the annual FTE and subsequent headcount columns should not be treated as contradictory observations of the same thing.
- **No grand total is presented:** the dates, FTE conventions, and organizational coverage differ, and OFAC’s verified figure is much older. Adding these figures would not give a reliable current total of full-time federal financial-regulatory employees.

## 4. Scope boundaries

**FINRA is important but is not a federal government agency**: it is a private, nonprofit self-regulatory organization overseen by the SEC. The **PCAOB** is also a congressionally established nonprofit corporation under SEC oversight, rather than a Cabinet department or conventional federal agency. They are therefore excluded from the federal-agency staffing table. [29][30]

Insurance regulation in the United States is predominantly state-based. Treasury’s **Federal Insurance Office** has monitoring, policy, and international responsibilities, but should not be mistaken for a general federal replacement for state insurance regulators. [11][31]

The list also does not count the entire Treasury Department, the IRS, DOJ, or FTC workforce as financial-regulatory staff. These organizations have broader tax, prosecution, competition, or consumer-protection responsibilities. Nor are staff of regulated firms—such as Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac—employees of their regulator.
