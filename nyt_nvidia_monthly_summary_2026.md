The analysis also identified **18 distinct audio/video items: 6 positive and 12 negative**. These are separate from the written-article totals. Including them gives **129 identified editorial items: 33 positive and 96 negative**.

The scope covered all NYT sections, including news, features, Opinion, substantive newsletters such as DealBook, interactives and liveblogs. Articles had to discuss Nvidia or Huang substantively; shared-topic, competitor and export-control stories could qualify. Passing mentions, duplicate versions, translations and individual liveblog update cards were excluded. Each liveblog was counted once.

Classification used headlines and available abstracts or indexed excerpts:

- **Positive:** achievements, growth, promising developments, support or defense of Nvidia or Huang.
- **Negative:** criticism, adverse business developments, risks, investigations or alleged misconduct.
- **Low confidence:** mixed or largely neutral framing that nevertheless received a positive/negative label to meet the requested binary classification.

**Twenty written items have low-confidence labels.** The labels describe favorable or unfavorable framing toward the subjects. They do not establish factual accuracy, NYT endorsement or editorial bias; reporting an unfavorable event is not itself evidence of hostility.

In the retrieved November coverage, **65 of 76 written items (85.5%) were negative**. This is a finding about the collected sample. Uneven search coverage prevents a reliable estimate of how total publication volume or sentiment changed across the full period.

The source workbook contains the article-level titles, dates, sections, original URLs, classifications, confidence ratings, reasoning and supporting evidence links. Its monthly totals were checked when preparing this summary; no new article search was conducted.
